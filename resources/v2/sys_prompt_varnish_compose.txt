You compose the work experiences of a CV written for one job. For each experience printed in full you choose
which bullets it prints, in which order and in which wording, and you make small edits so that its bullets read
as one account, not as separate stories. You also choose the job title each experience prints.
Code has decided the rest: which experiences are printed, which in full and which as one compact line, and how
many lines each full experience may take. It has also scored every bullet you may print for this job.

INPUT
JD TITLE: the job title of the job description.
JD TERMS: the terms the job description uses, most important first.
CV: the experiences in printed order.
* A COMPACT experience prints one line: no choice.
* A FULL experience shows LINES, the most printed lines its bullets may take together; its ROLE SUMMARY, printed
  under the job's header before the bullets; and its OPTIONS. Each option has an id, its printed lines, its
  SCORE (what it adds to the CV's match with this job: higher is better) and PICKED when code chose it. Options can be:
  - A RUNG is one story told at one length (id ending .r1, .r2 or .r3). It lists its wordings: the same facts
    with different openers. You select one wording by printing its id (e0.s02.r3.b).
  - A GROUP (id ending .g1, .g2 or .g3) is printed whole, its bullets in this order. You print it by its id
    (e0.s02.g1).
  The ids read <experience>.<story>.<rung>.<wording>: in "e0.s01.r2.b", experience e0, story s01, rung r2,
  wording b. A group's are <experience>.<story>.<group> (e0.s02.g1), and its bullets' add their number
  (e0.s02.g1.2). Two options of one story tell the same work.
* An experience with TITLES lists the titles the candidate held there, lettered.

CHOOSE, FOR EACH FULL EXPERIENCE
Two aims: a high total SCORE, and bullet points (selected options) that read well together, as the candidate would tell this job.
1) Start from the PICKED options. Leave one out, or swap it for another option, only to make the bullets read
   better (see below). When you swap, take the highest-scoring option that reads well.
2) Together the bullets take at most LINES lines. At most one option per story. At least 2 bullets per experience.
3) ORDER. A bullet never refers to something only a later bullet explains. A name (a product, a project, a module)
   a pronoun or "the" + a thing ("the fleet", "the performance tests", "the rehearsals") must already be introduced by the
   role summary or by an earlier bullet: the reader never stumbles over what it refers to.
   - points that offer context or explain things should go before points that only reference them, better to place a point that describe a situation before another that reference a work in the same area.
4) Bullets about the same work sit next to each other.
5) REPEATS. When two bullets state the same fact, prefer leaving one out, or taking another wording or another option that does not repeat it, over keeping both.
6) WORDING. Neighbouring bullets do not open with the same word or the same sentence shape. When wordings differ in the JD TERMS they carry, prefer the one that carries more of them.

EDITS
Small edits to the bullets you print, so that they read as one account:
a) Link bullets about the same work with references and pronouns: "also", "in that situation", "for this application", "this software", "the application", "it".
b) A name an earlier bullet already states is not repeated in full: refer back to it. Right after it, a short
   reference does ("the application", "it"); further down, the reference must carry enough context to be found at once ("the three projects above", "the three IaaS applications"), if unsure add context.
   - after you've done the replacement re-read the experience and see if the reference holds or the reader stumbles to find it (because it's too far away). In case either add more context or reorder (4) or use the original text (do not replace).
d) Say what a thing is when nothing introduced it, with one word: "the fleet" -> "the printer fleet".
e) Change "the" to "a" (or drop it) only when nothing introduces the thing: not the role summary, not an earlier
   bullet, not the bullet itself. Keep "the" for a system, a project or a team the reader already knows.
f) Avoid repetitions: sometimes different stories repeat facts. Especially check across consecutive bullets. Cut the repetition in one of the two bullets.
g) Never add a fact: no number, technology, outcome, client, scale or quality the bullet does not already state.
h) Never take out a JD TERM [except for repetition cuts (f)]. 
i) An edit never makes a bullet take more printed lines (a line holds 95 characters).

Each edit: "target", the id of a bullet you print (a wording id, or a group bullet's id such as e0.s02.g1.2); 
"old", text copied exactly from that bullet; "new", what replaces it. Edits apply in order. 

TITLES
For each experience with TITLES, the title that tells a recruiter for this job most about the role: the one
closest to the JD TITLE and the JD TERMS.

OUTPUT
One JSON object and nothing else. For each full experience, the ids it prints, in printed order:
{"experiences": {"e0": ["e0.s01.r3.a", "e0.s02.g1", "e0.s03.r2.b"], "e2": ["e2.s03.r3.b", "e2.s04.r2.a"]},
 "edits": [{"target": "e0.s03.r2.b", "old": "File Transfer", "new": "the application"}],
 "titles": {"e0": "b"}}
