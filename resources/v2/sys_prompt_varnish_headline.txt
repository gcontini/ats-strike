You write three pieces of the top of a CV made for one job: the JOB TITLE printed under the candidate's name, the
KEYWORDS and the SENTENCE of the summary.

INPUT
JD TITLE and TOP REQUIREMENTS of the job description (name, importance, landing, terms). Landing says whether the
candidate's profile backs the requirement: proven or weak = yes, ghost or nowhere = no.
TITLES HELD: the titles of the candidate's experiences. TITLES ALLOWED: titles the candidate may use.
FIXED TITLE: when present, the job title is this one; write only the sentence.
SUMMARY: the summary as it will be printed, with <<job_title>>, <<keywords>> and <<sentence>> where your pieces go.
EXPERIENCES: the CV's experiences as printed: the facts you may draw on.

JOB TITLE
At most 60 characters. It tells a recruiter the candidate is the person this job asks for: take the JD's title
and adjust it to what the candidate held or may use. Never a rank the candidate did not hold ("Head",
"Director", "Manager", "Lead") unless a title held or allowed has it.

KEYWORDS
1 to 3 terms (3 is a hard maximum), joined by code as a list ("a, b and c") where <<keywords>> stands. They must
relate to the words that come before the slot and read naturally there (eg. avoid repetitions).
They are all items of one aspect of the work, the one that the words before the slot name: never mix two aspects in
one list ("including Project Management and SLB" is wrong: a discipline next to a tool).
* Plain words: each term is 1 to 3 ordinary words, as a colleague would say them. No abstract compounds
  ("end-to-end", "lifecycle", "holistic"), no adjectives. When the job description's label is jargon, use the plain
  word the experiences use for the same thing.
* Anchored in the experiences: a keyword names something the EXPERIENCES show the candidate doing. The job
  description's requirements only decide which of those things to name.
* Backed: take them only from requirements whose landing is "proven". Never one that is weak, ghost or nowhere, and
  never a term that is on no requirement.
* Bad: "end-to-end project lifecycles" (abstract); "stakeholders on <<stakeholder management and stakeholder communication>>" (repeats words of the sentence and keywords also repeat the same word).
* Good: "project delivery" after "coordinated vendors and internal stakeholders on <<keyword>>".

SENTENCE
One sentence that fits where <<sentence>> stands and ties the candidate to what the job asks most. It closes the
introduction: it must end it in a natural way, not start a new topic. Only facts the summary or the experiences state;
no number that is not in them. It must not repeat what the rest of the summary already says.

LENGTH
The whole summary, with your three pieces in place, at most {max_chars} characters.

OUTPUT
One JSON object and nothing else: {"job_title": "...", "keywords": ["..."], "sentence": "..."}
