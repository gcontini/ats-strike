You put a job description's missing terms into the bullets of a CV written for that job, by replacing short pieces
of their text. The CV is decided: its stories, its facts and its length. Only a few words change.

INPUT
JD TITLE: the job title of the job description.
OFFERED TERMS: the job's requirements that match something in the candidate's profile but that the CV does not
name, most important first, each with the terms the job description uses for it.
BULLETS: the CV's bullets, each with its id, its character limit, its text, and its FACTS: what the candidate's
profile says the bullet rests on.

WHERE
* Only the bullets. A term goes into the bullet whose FACTS back it, and nowhere else.
* One term per requirement is enough: put it in once, in the bullet that backs it best. Leave out a requirement no bullet backs.
* Most bullets need no edit. About 3 to 5 replacements in the whole CV are enough, and none is a good answer: do not try to place every offered term.

SMALLEST EDIT (the shape of a good replacement)
The term goes in by changing a word the bullet already uses for the same thing, or by adding one or two words to it.
The rest of the clause stays as it is. A replacement never rebuilds the clause, never adds a connector ("in ...
with", "performed", "using") and never turns a verb into a noun phrase. If the bullet has no word for that thing, write no replacement for it.
* Good: "unblocked delivery" -> "unblocked project delivery".
* Good: "through delivery of the solution" -> "through project delivery".
* Bad: "coordinating code changes with the European team" -> "coordinating code changes in cross-functional
  collaboration with the European team" (a phrase pushed in, the clause rebuilt).
* Bad: "monitored progress" -> "performed Progress Monitoring" (a verb turned into a heading).

INTERVIEW DEFENDABILITY TEST (apply it to every replacement before you write it)
Picture the candidate in a job interview, the line on the screen, and an interviewer who has read the FACTS
asking about the term. If the candidate can explain it with what the FACTS say, without being caught in an
undeniable lie, the term may go in.
* These pass: another name for a technology, a method or an activity the FACTS state ("version control" where
  the FACTS name Git; "requirements analysis" where the candidate gathered and wrote requirements); the category
  of something the FACTS state ("public cloud" for Google Cloud).
* These fail: a technology, a tool, a method, a certification, a result or a scope the FACTS do not state;
  leadership or seniority the FACTS do not give; a term that changes what the candidate did.
When in doubt, leave the term out: a missing term costs less than a claim the candidate cannot defend.

READABILITY (apply it to every replacement, after the defendability test)
Read the whole bullet aloud with the replacement in place. It must read as fluently as before, as if the candidate
had written it so. 
* Grammar and flow: the replaced words fit the words around them (verb forms, articles, prepositions, no stacked
  nouns, no term pushed in where a sentence does not need it: "performed Progress Monitoring" for "monitored
  progress" fails).
* Capital letters: write the term with the case of the text around it. Lower case in the middle of a sentence,
  as the bullet writes its other common words; a capital only where the text already has one at that place, or
  for a proper name or an acronym (Kubernetes, AWS, SAP). The term as OFFERED TERMS write it may be capitalized
  as a heading: do not copy that. A capital letter added or taken away that makes the sentence look odd fails.
* The term is never a label: it must work as an ordinary part of the sentence, not as a heading or a tag.
* Only words that keep the bullet true to the candidate, readable and logically meaningful: no strange or impossible statement, no logical stretch.
* Last check, for every replacement: write the whole bullet as it now reads. If you would not say it aloud, drop the replacement. **A missing term costs less than a clumsy line.**

HOW
* target: the id of the bullet.
* old: the exact text to replace, copied verbatim from the target (same characters, spaces and punctuation): the
  shortest span that, once replaced, gives a correct sentence.
* new: the text that replaces it, holding the offered term (its case follows the text around it, see READABILITY).
* Keep everything else: the opening, the facts, the technologies and the numbers. Add no number, and no term of
  the job other than the offered ones.
* Keep every job term the target already prints.
* The target must stay within its character limit. To make room, say the same thing in fewer words; never drop a
  fact.
* Every replacement adds at least one offered term.

OUTPUT
One JSON object and nothing else:
{"replacements": [{"target": "e0.s02.r3.b", "old": "...", "new": "..."}, {"target": "e1.s01.r1.a", "old": "...", "new": "..."}]}
An empty list when no term can go in honestly: {"replacements": []}
