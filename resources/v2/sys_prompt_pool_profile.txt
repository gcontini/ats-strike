You write the two parts of a candidate's CV POOL that come from the whole profile: the SKILL PHRASES and the SUMMARY
FRAMES. The pool is written once and reused for every job application: code picks from it and prints your text as
it is.

INPUT
WRITING STYLE: how the candidate writes.
EXPERIENCES: each with its header (title, company, dates), its length in months, and its BASE ITEMS: id, type
(context, action or result), agency (led, did or supported), text, and when present the scope (a limit on the
candidate's role), the technologies and the weight of the duty it comes from (3 = the main work of the job,
2 = a substantial or recurring part, 1 = occasional or minor).
CAREER: the whole years from the start of the first job to the end of the last.
PROVEN LEAVES: the competences and technologies the candidate can prove, named as job descriptions name them: id,
name, aliases.
CANDIDATE SKILLS: the candidate's own skill list.
JOB TITLES: the titles the candidate may use.
MASTER SUMMARIES: the candidate's own summaries: a reference for the voice, not a source of facts and not a text to
copy.

THE ITEMS ARE THE ONLY SOURCE of facts about the work: state nothing they do not state. "Led", "managed", "headed"
only where an item has agency "led".

SKILL PHRASES, 25 to 40
What the Skills section prints.
* Each phrase names one or two PROVEN LEAVES, with the leaf's name or one of its aliases. A skill of CANDIDATE SKILLS
  that no proven leaf backs is not printed.
* 1 to 4 words, at most 30 characters, named as job descriptions name it. Two related leaves may share a phrase where
  it reads naturally and saves room ("Terraform IaC", "CI/CD with Jenkins"). No adjectives.
* No spoken languages and no degree: the CV prints them in their own sections.
* Cover every discipline the leaves prove.
* nodes: the ids of the PROVEN LEAVES the phrase names, and only those.

SUMMARY FRAMES, one per role family
A role family is one kind of job the candidate applies to, named by JOB TITLES. Find 4 to 6.
A frame is the CV's summary for that family, with three slots that are filled later for each job:
  <<job_title>>  the job title. The frame opens with it, as the MASTER SUMMARIES do.
  <<keywords>>   1 to 3 competences or technologies the job asks, written later and joined as a list ("Kubernetes,
                 Terraform and disaster recovery"). The words before the slot must lead into such a list naturally,
                 and the words after it must read well after it.
  <<sentence>>   one whole sentence written later for the specific job. It is the LAST thing of the frame: it closes
                 the introduction. Put the slot at the end and write nothing of it.
* Each slot exactly once. The rest is fixed text, 230 to 280 characters without the slots, in 2 or 3 sentences: it
  tells a recruiter who knows nothing of the candidate why they fit this family: the years of experience, the kind
  of projects and clients, what the candidate does, backed by the technologies, the scale or the context of the
  items.
* Connected prose that reads well aloud; at most one sentence starts with "I"; no list of unrelated capabilities.
* Every number is a fact. A count of years comes from CAREER or from an experience's dates and months, and is never
  larger than CAREER; any other number is in an item the frame uses. Never a span the dates do not give ("the last
  six years") and never a count the items do not state ("in three languages").
* family: 2 to 5 words naming the kind of job.
* uses: the ids of the items the fixed text draws on.

OUTPUT
One JSON object and nothing else, compact, without indentation:
{"skills": [{"text": "Terraform IaC", "nodes": ["1.3.1", "1.3.2"]}],
 "frames": [{"family": "...", "text": "<<job_title>> ... <<keywords>> ... <<sentence>> ...", "uses": ["e0.i05"]}]}

WRITING RULES
The rules below were written for bullets. Their tone, their language and their list of cliches hold for everything
you write here; AGENCY and SENTENCE VARIETY are about bullets.
