You decompose ONE work experience of a candidate's master profile into BASE ITEMS, STORIES and DUTY WEIGHTS.
CV bullets will later be written from your output alone, without going back to the profile:
nothing may be lost, and nothing may be added.

INPUT
EXPERIENCE: a JSON object with title, company, dates, context, role_summary and duties.
Duties are numbered from 0, in the order given.

BASE ITEMS
* One base item is ONE statement: one thing that was the case, one thing the candidate did, or one outcome.
  Split a duty that tells several things into several items. Never merge statements of different duties
  into one item.
* text: the statement in the profile's own words, past tense, readable on its own. Resolve every reference
  to another duty ("the projects above", "the implementation above", "there", "the point above") by naming
  what it refers to. Add NOTHING the profile does not say: no number, no technology, no outcome, no adjective.
* source: the span or spans the item comes from, copied VERBATIM, character by character, from that duty
  (or from role_summary). Each span is one contiguous piece of the text. Together they hold everything the
  item states.
* type:
  - "context": a fact about the situation: the system, the client, the team, the constraint, the scale,
    why it mattered.
  - "action": what the candidate did.
  - "result": what came of it: an outcome, a measure, a recognition, a reaction of the client.
* agency:
  - "led": the profile says the candidate led, managed, owned, was responsible for or guided it.
  - "did": the candidate did the work personally.
  - "supported": the candidate collaborated on, contributed to or assisted work that others led.
  - "": for a context item.
* scope: a limit the profile states on the candidate's role, in the profile's words (for example a note
  saying what the candidate did NOT lead); otherwise "". A note that only states such a limit is never an
  item of its own: it becomes the scope of the item it limits.
* tech: the technologies, tools, methods and standards the item names, spelled as the profile spells them.
* tags: 1 to 4 competences the item proves, as short noun phrases a recruiter would search for
  ("disaster recovery", "vendor management", "Kubernetes operations"). Context items take the tags of the
  work they frame.
* Every sentence of every duty is covered by at least one item. The role_summary gives context items only.

EXPERIENCE CONTEXT
* A context item belongs to the whole experience, not to a story, when it frames the job: the employer, the
  team, the mandate, the system the job is about, its scale. It belongs there too when items of two or more
  stories presuppose it. Give it "story": 0. The CV's role summary will be written from these items.
* A context item about one piece of work stays in that piece's story.

STORIES
* A story is ONE piece of work: one system or programme, in one phase. It holds that work's own context,
  its actions and its results.
* Work done inside a piece of work belongs to its story. When a duty says it happened during a programme
  (for example "during the migration, I also rebuilt the reporting scripts"), it belongs to that programme's
  story, even when the profile tells it in a duty of its own.
* Do NOT cut stories where the profile cuts its duties: one duty may tell several pieces of work, and one
  piece of work may run over several duties.
* A story too large to tell in a few lines, more than about 10 action and result items, is split by phase
  (for example the design, then the rollout).
* Every action and result item belongs to exactly one story. Every story has at least one action or result
  item. An action that stands alone is a story of one.
* title: 3 to 8 words naming the piece of work.
* anchor: the 1 or 2 items that say what the story is about, usually the context item that names the system
  and the main action. Every CV bullet about the story will state its anchor, so together they must say
  what the work was without the other items.

DUTY WEIGHTS
* Give every duty a weight: its importance in the candidate's career.
  - 3: the main work of the job, done for most of it, or what the candidate is known for.
  - 2: a substantial or recurring part of the job.
  - 1: occasional, one-off or minor.
  The two ends: eight years of building one library is a 3; one presentation of a cloud platform to
  management is a 1.
* Judge only from what the profile says: the dates, words about duration or frequency, the role summary.
  When unsure, give 2.
* why: a few words saying what the weight rests on.

OUTPUT
One JSON object and nothing else, compact, without indentation:
{"items": [{"n": 1, "duty": 0, "type": "action", "text": "...", "source": ["..."], "agency": "did",
            "scope": "", "tech": ["..."], "tags": ["..."], "story": 1}],
 "stories": [{"n": 1, "title": "...", "anchor": [3, 4]}],
 "duties": [{"duty": 0, "weight": 3, "why": "..."}]}
In an item, "duty" is the duty's number, or -1 for the role_summary, and "story" is the story's n, or 0 for
experience context. "anchor" lists items by their n. "duties" has one entry per duty.
