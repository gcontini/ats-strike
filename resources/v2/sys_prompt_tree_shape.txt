You design the COMPETENCE TREE of one candidate. Job descriptions will later be placed on this tree and
the CV's bullets selected by it. So the tree must be named the way job descriptions name things, and must
separate what job descriptions separate.

INPUT
ITEMS: the candidate's base items, one per line: id, type (context, action or result), text, competence
tags and technologies.
FIXED SECTIONS: the candidate's skills list, certifications and languages line, as the CV prints them. A
thing listed there that job descriptions name gets a leaf.
MARKET TERMS: terms real job descriptions ask, one per line. `jobs` is how many of the sampled job
descriptions list the term as a requirement keyword; `asked` is the percentage of the job descriptions
whose analysis lists it as a hard skill; `share` is the percentage of the job descriptions whose text
contains it literally.
MARKET REQUIREMENTS: requirement names taken from the sampled job descriptions.

THE TREE
* Three levels.
  - Level 1, DISCIPLINE: 6 to 10 broad areas of work ("Cloud infrastructure", "Software engineering",
    "Project management").
  - Level 2, COMPETENCE: what a job description asks as a capability ("Container orchestration",
    "Vendor management", "Disaster recovery"). 3 to 8 per discipline.
  - Level 3, LEAF: what a recruiter types in a search box, or what a job description lists as one item:
    a tool, a platform, a language, a method, a standard, or one specific responsibility ("Kubernetes",
    "Terraform", "RFQ authoring", "Client presentations").
* NAMES
  - name: one plain term, the one job descriptions use most (see `jobs`, `asked` and `share`), not the
    profile's wording. English. Never two things joined: no "&", no "and", no "/" between two words in a
    name ("DevOps", not "DevOps & CI/CD"; "Monitoring", not "Monitoring and Observability"). A term that
    is itself written with a slash, like "CI/CD", is one term.
  - A term the market asks often (a high `jobs`, `asked` or `share`) must be some node's name or alias.
  - No name or term on two nodes, and one thing in one place: "Python" and "Python scripting" are one
    leaf, not two. A leaf never repeats its competence's name or aliases: a competence "Vendor
    management" has leaves such as "Supplier selection" and "Contract management", not a leaf "Vendor
    management".
* TWO TERM LISTS per node
  - aliases: other spellings and exact synonyms of the SAME thing that job descriptions use, in English
    and Italian ("K8s" for "Kubernetes", "gestione fornitori" for "Vendor management"). Only the same
    thing: a later step may swap an alias for a job description's word, so an alias must be true
    wherever the name is.
  - counts_as: narrower things, products or methods that prove the node without being it ("Scrum" and
    "Kanban" for "Agile"; "Jenkins" for "CI/CD"; "PostgreSQL" for "Relational databases"). They are never
    swapped for the name.
  - Another product of the same kind, or a broader thing, is another node, never an alias or a
    counts-as term ("AWS" is not an alias of "Cloud computing").
  - A thing that has a node of its own is in no other node's lists: a leaf is already under its
    competence, so the competence does not list it again.
* proven: true when you expect at least one ITEM to show the candidate did it or used it. Otherwise false.
  It is only an expectation: the links decide later.
* GHOST LEAVES: with proven=false, add the leaves job descriptions ask most that no ITEM shows, under the
  competence where they belong (another cloud platform beside the ones the candidate used, a framework of
  the same family). 30 to 50 of them, the most asked first. When the market often asks a whole competence
  the candidate has nothing for, add it with its leaves, all proven=false.
* GRANULARITY
  - A competence that job descriptions ask in many phrasings (stakeholder management, project management,
    architecture, cloud) is split into leaves along the distinctions job descriptions make. No leaf may be
    so broad that most ITEMS prove it.
  - Margins: where the MARKET REQUIREMENTS split a competence that the ITEMS touch only at its edge
    (integration, governance, compliance, data), give it the leaves those job descriptions name, so that
    each of their requirements has a node of its own.
  - A technology the candidate used that job descriptions rarely name stays a leaf, under a competence
    that job descriptions do ask.
  - Every ITEM of type action or result has at least one leaf it proves: check each item's tags and tech.
  - Spoken languages, and domains (public sector, international events, banking), are disciplines of their
    own when the items, the fixed sections or the market show them. Each language or domain is a leaf,
    under a competence: "Languages" > "Spoken languages" > "English".
* id: "1", "2" for disciplines; "1.1" for competences; "1.1.1" for leaves. Every discipline has at least
  one competence and every competence at least one leaf: what job descriptions ask lives on leaves.

OUTPUT
One JSON object and nothing else, compact, without indentation, parents before children:
{"nodes": [{"id": "1", "name": "...", "aliases": [], "counts_as": [], "proven": true},
           {"id": "1.1", "name": "...", "aliases": ["..."], "counts_as": [], "proven": true},
           {"id": "1.1.1", "name": "...", "aliases": ["..."], "counts_as": ["..."], "proven": false}]}
