You write the BULLET POOL of one work experience of a candidate's CV. The pool is written once and reused for every
job application: for each job, code picks which stories to show and at which length, and prints your text as it is,
under the job's header and its role summary. So every bullet must be true to the items, understandable on its own,
and good enough to print.

INPUT
WRITING STYLE: how the candidate writes.
EXPERIENCE: title, company and dates of the job: the header printed above the bullets.
ROLE SUMMARY: the line printed under the header, before the bullets, and the ids of the items it states.
SETTING: every context item of this job, whichever story it sits in: the team, the client, the system, the scale.
STORIES: the stories to write. Each has its id, its title, LONG and MERGED when it is, its ANCHOR (the items that
say what the story is about) and its BASE ITEMS: id, type (context, action or result), agency (led, did or
supported), text, and when present the scope (a limit on the candidate's role) and the technologies.

THE ITEMS ARE THE ONLY SOURCE
* A bullet states only what the items listed in its `uses` state. No number, technology, outcome, client, scale or
  quality that is not in one of them.
* "Led", "managed", "headed", "directed" only when one of those items has agency "led". An item with agency
  "supported" is told as work done with others ("worked with", "helped"), never as the candidate's own lead.
  A scope limit holds in every bullet that uses the item.
* Keep technologies, tools, methods and standards spelled as the items spell them.

THE READER HAS READ THE HEADER AND THE ROLE SUMMARY
* Never repeat what they say: not the company, not the job title, not the team, the client, the system or the
  mission the role summary introduces.
* Lean on them instead: "the platform" or "the client" is clear when the role summary introduced it. List each item
  of the role summary a bullet leans on in its `assumes`.
* Anything else a bullet refers to, it names. "The solution", "the new stack", "the project" name nothing unless the
  role summary or the bullet itself says which one.
* The role summary is all a bullet may lean on. A name the reader has not met (a product, a programme, a module) is
  explained in the bullet, with its item in `uses`, or left out: "the Atlas module" means nothing until the bullet
  says what Atlas does.

EVERY BULLET SAYS WHAT ITS STORY IS ABOUT
* Each ladder bullet, and the first bullet of each group, names the story's ANCHOR: its system, programme or main
  action, in a few words. Of a two-item anchor it names the one that says what the story is about; it need not
  restate both.
* So it uses, or assumes from the role summary, at least one ANCHOR item.

CONTEXT IS MERGED, NEVER ALONE
* A bullet is about something the candidate did or achieved: it draws on at least one action or result item. A
  context item never makes a bullet of its own.
* An action told without its context is a weak bullet. The reader knows only the header and the role summary: merge
  into the bullet the context that makes the action understandable and that the role summary does not give: which
  system, for which client or event, facing which problem, at what scale, to what purpose. Take it from the story's
  context items first, then from SETTING.
* Context belongs to the same work: one project's problem is not another project's. Take an item from SETTING only
  when it is about the work the bullet tells.
* A bullet stands alone: it will be printed without the other bullets of its story. It never refers to something
  only another bullet explains ("that cluster", "the system above"). Name the thing.

FOR EACH STORY, A LADDER
The same story at three lengths. Each rung is written for its length: it is not the longer one cut short.
* rung 1: one printed line, at most 95 characters. The core action, with the term a recruiter searches for and the
  least context that makes it understandable.
* rung 2: two printed lines, 130 to 190 characters. The action with its context, or with its result.
* rung 3: three printed lines, 225 to 285 characters. Context, action and result: the story in full.
* A story with too little to say for a rung stops at the rung below: never pad, never repeat, never add. Rung 1 is
  always written.
* Each rung in TWO wordings, "a" and "b": the same facts, with a different first word and a different sentence
  shape. They are alternatives: only one is printed.
* Count the characters, spaces included. A bullet over its limit is useless: it breaks the page.

GROUPS, ONLY FOR A STORY MARKED LONG
A long story has more to tell than rung 3 holds. Also write it as 1 to 3 GROUPS. A group is 2 or 3 bullets printed
together, in this order, that tell the whole story.
* Each group tells the whole story from a different angle: for instance for a technical reader (the system and how
  it was built), for a delivery or management reader (the plan, the team, the client), or outcome first. Write as
  many groups as give a genuinely different view; one is fine. A group is never one aspect of the story: each mixes
  context, action and result.
* Its first bullet names the ANCHOR. Here, and only here, a later bullet may lean on an earlier one of the same
  group ("During the port, ..."): list in its `assumes` the items of the earlier bullet it leans on.
* Each bullet at most 285 characters and still tells an action or a result; the group 4 to 7 printed lines in all
  (a line holds 95 characters), up to 9 for a story marked MERGED.
* One wording: the groups are the alternatives.
* In the reply, "groups" is one list of bullets, in printed order, each with its group number: 1, then 2, then 3.
* A story not marked LONG has no groups: its "groups" is [].

FOR EVERY BULLET
* uses: the ids of all the items it takes any fact from, SETTING items included.
* covers: among them, the action and result items whose whole statement a reader learns from this bullet. Never a
  context item.
* assumes: the ids of the items it leans on without stating them: items the role summary states and, in a group,
  items an earlier bullet of the group uses; nothing else. Never an id that is in its `uses`.

OUTPUT
One JSON object and nothing else, compact, without indentation:
{"stories": [{"id": "e1.s03",
  "ladder": [{"rung": 1, "wording": "a", "text": "...", "uses": ["e1.i20"], "covers": ["e1.i20"], "assumes": ["e1.i01"]},
             {"rung": 1, "wording": "b", "text": "...", "uses": ["e1.i19", "e1.i20"], "covers": ["e1.i20"], "assumes": []}],
  "groups": [{"group": 1, "text": "...", "uses": ["e1.i19", "e1.i20"], "covers": ["e1.i20"], "assumes": ["e1.i01"]},
             {"group": 1, "text": "...", "uses": ["e1.i21"], "covers": ["e1.i21"], "assumes": ["e1.i20"]},
             {"group": 2, "text": "...", "uses": ["e1.i20", "e1.i21"], "covers": ["e1.i20", "e1.i21"], "assumes": ["e1.i01"]},
