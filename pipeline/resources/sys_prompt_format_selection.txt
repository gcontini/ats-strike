Input:
* KEY POINTS: JSON list of the job's requirements. Each has id, requirement, keywords, importance (0-100). Item 1 is the job title.
* FORMATS: JSON object of the CV formats: for each one, how many work experiences it shows, the maximum duties of each
  experience, and the maximum duties overall. Every format fits the page: they trade duties for experiences.
  An experience can show 0 duties: it then shows only a short summary of what the candidate did there.
* WORK EXPERIENCES: JSON list from the candidate's profile, most recent first. Each duty ends with "|<scores>",
  e.g. "|3-80,7-40": the KEY POINTS it matches (by id), each with how well it matches (0-100).

Choose the CV format, the work experiences to show and how many duties each one shows. The most recent work experience
is always shown. Work in this order:
1. Most important duties first. Start from the format with the fewest experiences and fill its places one duty at a time,
   taking next the duty that adds the most: for each KEY POINT, add up its 2 best scores among the duties taken (the others
   do not count); a KEY POINT is full at 2 x its importance (importance 70: full at 140 points), and points beyond that add
   nothing. Each duty taken is one place for its experience. The experiences shown are the ones whose duties come first,
   up to the format's number of experiences. When no duty left adds points, fill the remaining places with the best of
   the others: the format's maximum overall is what fits the page, and a place left empty is empty space on it.
   Keep 1 or 2 of the format's places for the experiences whose duties taken are the longest: the writer may split a long
   duty in two.
2. Years covered. When the duties left add very little (they score low, or their KEY POINTS are already full), count the years
   the chosen experiences cover, from their dates, against the candidate's whole career. If a significant part of the
   career is not shown, switch to a format with more experiences and add the longest experience not shown yet.
   Its places come from your lowest-scoring duties: give it those places when its own duties score as well as them;
   otherwise it shows 0 duties. Decide what is significant, and whether one more switch is worth it, by weighing the
   completeness of the CV against the importance of the duties lost (never trade an important duty for a not so important experience).
3. duty_caps: how many duties each chosen experience shows, from 0 to the format's maximum, never more than the work
   experience has. Together they use all the format's maximum overall, unless the chosen experiences do not have that
   many duties.

Output only JSON, with the experience ids in profile order and one duty cap for each, in the same order:
{"reason": "<why this format and these experiences, with the years they cover, one or two sentences>", "format": "<A, B or C>", "experience_ids": [<ids, in profile order>], "duty_caps": [<one per id, same order>]}
